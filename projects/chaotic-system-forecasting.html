Chaotic System Forecasting

A scientific machine-learning project exploring forecasting horizons in chaotic dynamics with Echo State Networks and physics-informed reservoir computing.

Time SeriesReservoir ComputingPhysics-Informed MLOptimization

The Problem

Chaotic systems are difficult to forecast because tiny changes in initial conditions can lead to rapidly diverging trajectories. The project investigates how reservoir computing and physics-informed constraints can extend useful forecasting horizons.

The Approach

  • Lorenz-system simulation and forecasting experiments.
  • Conventional Echo State Networks (ESN) as a data-driven baseline.
  • Physics-Informed ESN (PI-ESN) with physical residuals derived from the governing dynamics.
  • Initial-condition experimentation and hyperparameter optimization with Optuna.
  • Evaluation in Lyapunov-time units to make the predictability horizon meaningful for chaotic dynamics.

Technical Decisions

  • The work uses Lyapunov time rather than only raw time to evaluate chaotic forecasting.
  • Paper-reported ESN and PI-ESN baselines are kept distinct from the achieved experimental result.
  • The project focuses on scientific reproducibility and careful forecasting diagnostics rather than a black-box score.

Results

Referenced baselines were approximately 4.5 Lyapunov Times for ESN and 5.5 for PI-ESN. The developed experimental approach reached 6.0 Lyapunov Times.

Why It Matters

This work shows the value of combining dynamical-system knowledge with data-driven forecasting when modeling highly sensitive nonlinear systems.
