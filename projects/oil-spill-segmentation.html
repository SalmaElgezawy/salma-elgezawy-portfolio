Oil Spill Semantic Segmentation

A multi-class semantic-segmentation project for aerial oil-spill imagery with class balancing, attention mechanisms, and a custom decoder.

Computer VisionSemantic SegmentationDeep LearningEnvironmental AI

The Problem

Oil-spill monitoring from aerial imagery requires detailed pixel-level interpretation. The task is complicated by class imbalance, strong visual similarity between categories, and high variation within the same class.

The Approach

  • Six-class semantic segmentation: Background, Emulsion, Oil, Oil Platform, Sheen, and Ship.
  • Image preprocessing and augmentation with geometric and photometric transforms.
  • Class-distribution analysis and class weighting / balancing strategies.
  • Pretrained ResNet50 encoder with a custom decoder, skip connections, progressive bilinear upsampling, and channel/spatial attention.
  • Weighted Cross-Entropy and Dice Loss with AdamW, learning-rate scheduling, and early stopping.

Technical Decisions

  • The pipeline explicitly addresses class imbalance rather than relying only on architecture changes.
  • Attention and skip connections help preserve spatial detail across encoder-decoder stages.
  • Qualitative segmentation evidence is more informative here than over-emphasizing a single score.

Results

The project demonstrates a full semantic-segmentation workflow from dataset analysis through training and qualitative prediction review.

Why It Matters

It shows how deep learning can be applied to pixel-level environmental imagery where different surface phenomena may appear visually similar.
